Summary

The baseline assessment looks at how transportation and communication held up in past emergencies, mostly the 2021 Caldor Fire and recent winter storms, and sets the starting point for the transportation communications strategy.i It draws on more than 50 plans and studies and interviews with transit, public works, and emergency management staff.

Bottom line The basin's emergency plans and command structure are sound, and the road agencies fit into them well. What breaks down is around them: transit with no set role, information passed by personal contacts and group texts, no evacuation traffic plan, equipment that fails without power or cell service, and people without a car who are hard to reach.

Background

Who does what in an emergency

Five groups of agencies share the work. Under the national incident command system, a unified command brings them together when an incident crosses jurisdictions.i

GroupRole in an emergencyExamples
Emergency managementLeads coordination: runs emergency operations centers, sets priorities, and arranges shelters, staging areas, and transportation resources.Cal OES, Nevada Division of Emergency Management, county emergency managers
FireFights the fire, reports conditions, and with law enforcement recommends evacuation warnings and orders.CAL FIRE, Nevada Division of Forestry, U.S. Forest Service, Tahoe Fire and Fuels Team, local fire districts
Law enforcementIssues and carries out evacuation orders, controls traffic and access, and can request transit for people who need a ride.CHP, Nevada State Police, county sheriffs, local police
Transportation operationsRun the evacuation highways and local roads: closures, traffic control, and road condition information.Caltrans, NDOT, county and city public works
Transit and mobilityMove people without a car with buses, paratransit, and shuttles, under the direction of emergency officials.Tahoe Transportation District, TART, South Shore TMA, Truckee North Tahoe TMA, Mountaineer Transit

School districts (bus fleets), resorts and hotels, social service and community groups, and utilities are key partners outside these five groups.

How the assessment was done

Findings are organized around five focus areas: transportation response coordination, transportation communication, evacuation planning and execution, technology, and community preparedness.

  • Plans and studies. More than 50 documents: hazard mitigation, emergency operations, and evacuation plans and studies, plus case studies from the basin and elsewhere.
  • Technical interviews. Four group interviews, June 29 to July 24, 2026, with 13 organizations: the Tahoe Transportation District, South Shore TMA, Barton Health, Cal Poly, South Lake Tahoe Public Works, El Dorado County Engineering, Tahoe City PUD, Placer County OES and Public Works, TART, Mountaineer Transit, Truckee Public Works, and the Truckee North Tahoe TMA. Caltrans sent written input, and earlier TSMO Plan interviews with Caltrans and NDOT were used.
  • Oral histories. 20 long-form interviews with first responders, emergency managers, community leaders, and residents in October 2026, still being added.
  • Surveys. The project's public survey (results still being added) and Placer County's evacuation study survey.

Limits. After-action reports and incident data could not be obtained, so the findings rest mostly on interviews and describe what participants reported. Fire and law enforcement agencies were not in the four technical interviews, and freight has not been covered yet.
