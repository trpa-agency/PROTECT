  • Susceptibility (E1). The USGS Landslide Susceptibility Index, classed on the draft's mapping: very high (5) is 3, high (4) is 2, moderate (3) is 1, low and very low are 0.
  • Recorded landslides. Every mapped landslide in the USGS inventory (California Geological Survey and Nevada Bureau of Mines and Geology in the basin) counts as class 3. Polygons over 10 km2 are mapping units, not slides, and are left out; a landslide point counts within 50 m. Both are TRPA choices the rubric leaves open.
  • Climate term (E2). Cal-Adapt precipitation change at weight 0.2 scores 0 at the historical horizon by definition, so the score is 0.5 x E1 / 0.7 and tops out at 2.1 until the 2050 and 2080 columns are added.
  • Sampling. Roads take the highest class touching the segment, with the share of length in each class in the table. Bridges take the highest class within 25 m. Culverts take the highest class anywhere in their drainage area, which is why a culvert in town can score 3 when the slope above it does.
  • Not modeled. Where a slide would run. The scores say how prone the ground is, not what it would hit.
  • Not yet scored. Sensitivity: pavement condition (roads), size and condition (culverts), span type, condition, and span length (bridges) sit at the draft defaults.
