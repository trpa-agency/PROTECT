Pair

Equation and scale

Vpair = Σ (weight × score) over the pair's indicators. The draft's tables score most indicators in half-point steps (0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3); a few score whole points only. Indicator weights sum to 1.0 within a pair, so the pair score is on the same 0 to 3 scale. The exposure weight wE is the sum of the exposure indicator weights and wS the sum of the sensitivity weights, which is the draft's Vulnerability = (Exposure Score × wE) + (Sensitivity Score × wS) written per component. Epair and Spair are the weight-normalized component scores (each 0 to 3) so the tool can show them separately.
Several of the draft's sensitivity rubrics have a floor of 1 rather than 0 (bridge condition, pavement condition, culvert condition, paved surface, scour codes 5 to 9), so a sensitivity component is rarely 0. This is the draft's design and is kept.

Flood exposure source TRPA proposal

TRPA is not acquiring a flood depth model. Flood exposure is scored from FEMA flood zones and stream crossings on a four-value scale (1 percent zone 3; 0.2 percent zone or mapped TRPA stream 2; NHD flowline outside a zone 1; none 0). The draft's depth-based rows for culverts and bridges are listed for the record and do not apply. The culvert loading ratio carries the hydraulic signal. Decision 19.

Vulnerability classes TRPA proposal

V is kept to two decimals and classed for the map and posters: Low under 1.0, Medium 1.0 to under 2.0, High 2.0 and over. Breaks live in config.yaml (scoring.v_breaks). Decision 9.

Criticality

Component 1 of the assessment, scored separately, not a multiplier. Road segments carry a 0 to 3 class (C_class); bridges and culverts inherit it through parent_segment_id. Priority is read on two axes, vulnerability class by criticality class, and the hotspot lists rank assets by V within the High criticality class first.

Aggregation across pairs TRPA proposal

One asset appears in several pairs (a culvert carries FL-C, LS-C, DF-C). The asset-level rating is the maximum pair score, V_max, with V_max_pair recording which hazard set it. Summing would double-count one physical failure at one location. Lane default confirmed Oct. 8; decision 9 with the consultant.

Horizons

Historical, mid-century (2050), and late-century (2080). The Nov. 9 workshop uses the historical horizon only. Field names carry a horizon suffix from the start so December adds columns, not a schema change. Sensitivity does not vary by horizon. The FEMA flood term has no horizon; the flood pairs carry the future through the culvert loading ratio, whose design flows take 2050 and 2080 factors from the climate pipeline.

Missing data

The draft leaves most defaults TBD. Where it states one (pavement fair, surface paved, bridge condition none scores 1, scour other scores 1) that value is used; the rest are TRPA proposals (decision 8). An asset scored on any default carries S_source (or E_source) = partial; one with every sensitivity indicator defaulted carries default.

Sampling rule

Line assets take the length-weighted maximum of the hazard surface along the segment. Point assets (bridges, transit centers) take the maximum within a 25 m buffer. Small culverts are the exception: surfaces that describe what arrives at the crossing (flood, debris flow, landslide) are sampled over the culvert's contributing watershed, with the 25 m buffer as the fallback where no watershed could be delineated (decision 3).

Asset sets

Small culverts (FL-C, LS-C, DF-C)

  • Everything in the Culverts layer typed culvert; stormwater pipes, inlets, and basins are inventory only.
  • Road-crossing rule applied to El Dorado and Washoe pipes: a pipe that crosses a street centerline is a culvert unless both ends are manholes.
  • Within 25 m of an NBI culvert-type structure: excluded, scores with the bridge.
  • No parent segment within 50 m: inventory only, no rating (decision 10).
  • NDOT assets score in the analysis geodatabase only; nothing asset-level is published without NDOT review.
  • Expected scored set: about 5,000 including NDOT.

Bridges and large culverts (FL-B, LS-B, DF-B)

  • The 40 National Bridge Inventory structures inside the TRPA boundary, built by scripts/build_bridges.py.
  • Five are NBI culvert-type structures (item 43B = 19, spans over 20 ft) and score here, not with small culverts.
  • NBI condition items run 0 (failed) to 9 (excellent); the draft's bands are 0 to 2, 3 to 4, 5, 6, and 7 to 9 or N.
  • Structures not over a waterway stay in the set: water-dependent sensitivity indicators score at the draft's lowest band.

Road segments (DF-R, AV-R)

  • Streets_Network_Tahoe segments, scored length-weighted maximum along the segment.
  • Segment IDs and C_class come from the network lane; this lane supplies the debris-flow surface and the DF-R scores so DF-R, DF-B, and DF-C share one surface and one set of class breaks.
  • Avalanche: exposure only, current terrain, every segment scored. The corridor list (US-50, SR-89, SR-28, SR-207, SR-431) is the sanity check, not the scope.

Culvert loading ratio: how Qevent and Qcap are estimated

The draft's Qdesign is missing for nearly every culvert, so capacity is estimated from geometry. Parameters live in config.yaml and the method runs in scripts/culvert_profile.py.

Watershed

Flow direction and accumulation on the TRPA hydro-enforced bare-earth lidar DEM (2 m, EPSG 26910) at native resolution on the server machine. Sinks shallower than 3 m are filled before flow direction; deeper sinks (lakes, real basins) are kept. Pour point is the maximum-accumulation cell within 20 m of the culvert on the upstream side of the road. Culverts within 10 m of each other on the same segment are one crossing.

Event flow, Qevent

Rational method, Q = C i A, for basins under 1 sq mi, with C by majority landcover and i the Atlas 14 intensity at the basin's time of concentration (Kirpich); 100-yr headline, 25-yr carried (decision 5). Basins of 1 sq mi and over use the USGS regional regression equations. 2050 and 2080 flows apply the climate pipeline's DDF change factors to i. The local computation replaces the draft's StreamStats API call so NDOT crossings are not sent to a third-party service.

Capacity, Qcap

FHWA HDS-5 inlet-control equation solved for Q at HW/D = 1.0, coefficients by shape, material, and inlet type (decision 6). Underestimates capacity by roughly 10 to 20 percent against the nomographs, which is conservative for a screen; Manning full flow is carried as the cross-check. Barrels at one crossing are summed before the ratio.

Known limits: the rational method is a screening estimate, slopes and roughness are assumed, and culvert inverts are not surveyed. The ratio is for ranking, not design. The future-horizon factor scales rainfall intensity only, so rain-on-snow winter peaks are not captured; 2050 and 2080 ratios are a lower bound for mid-elevation basins.

Validation checks before delivery

Culverts

  • Known-failure check: the Aug. 2025 Incline Village blocked culvert (FL-C S3), the June 2026 Washoe County flash flood crossings (loading ratio at the 1-hr intensity), and the SR 89 Meeks Creek culvert (scores under the bridge rubric, the cross-check between the two paths) should land High.
  • Distribution checks on contributing area and loading ratio by jurisdiction; 20 watersheds checked by eye against the hillshade.
  • Report how many culverts change class between the 25-yr and 100-yr ratio.

Bridges

  • Meeks Creek (structure 25 0019; culvert condition 5, scour code 4) should land High or Medium on FL-B.
  • With 40 structures, review every row by hand against the NBI record before delivery.

Avalanche

  • The five corridors should carry nearly all High segments; list any High segment off those corridors for the DOTs.
  • The score describes closure likelihood, not damage.
