Draft for Workshop 1, Nov. 9, 2026 This guide goes with the Results page of the PROTECT vulnerability assessment: how every road, culvert, bridge, path, and transit hub got its score, how to read the map, and what each field in the page's popups, tables, and CSV exports means. The results are a draft for workshop review and will change as inputs are finalized (the contracted fire and soil burn severity data, the USGS debris-flow model, the 2050 and 2080 horizons, and owner review of the culvert records). The scores rank assets and raise questions; they are not design values.

Printing to PDF is the download: letter size, two pages. Choose Save as PDF as the destination.

How an asset gets a score three steps, built on FHWA vulnerability assessment guidance

Step 1

Criticality: how much the asset matters

Each road segment is scored on who depends on it (equity), traffic, the detour if it closed, and access to critical places, then cut into quarters, 0 (lowest) to 3 (top). Culverts and bridges take the class of the road they carry. Criticality sits beside vulnerability and is never multiplied into it.

Step 2

Vulnerability: how likely the asset is to fail

Fifteen hazard-asset pairs are screened as Vulnerability = (Exposure x wE) + (Sensitivity x wS). Exposure is how much of the hazard reaches the asset; sensitivity is how badly it would fare. Indicators score 0 to 3 and the weights sum to 1 per pair, so a pair score is 0 to 3. Roads carry no sensitivity: a road's score is its exposure.

Step 3

Disruption scenarios: what happens when it fails

For places that are both critical and vulnerable, the RA2CE model (Deltares) runs event scenarios on the street network: detours, travel time, access to key destinations, and people cut off. This step follows the workshop and is not on the Results page yet.

Classes. Low under 1, Medium 1 to under 2, High 2 and over. Wildfire scores run 1 to 3 everywhere, so wildfire uses Low under 2, Medium 2 to 2.5, High over 2.5.
Combined class. An asset in several pairs carries its highest pair score (the popup names it as Highest hazard); its class is the highest of its pair classes.
Hotspots. High vulnerability on a road in the top criticality quarter. The Hotspots only switch narrows the Overview to them.
Stand-in. A dataset used in place of the one the method names, on the same breaks, until the named one arrives. The debris-flow pairs are the stand-in pairs (fire severity for soil burn severity, and a drainage-size break that puts nearly every basin at 3), so they stay out of the combined class; the "with stand-ins" fields include them.
Missing data. An unobserved sensitivity input (most culvert conditions) takes the method's default, and Sensitivity data reads partial or default.
Horizon. Every score is historical (the _hist suffix in exports). The 2050 and 2080 columns come in December.

Reading the map

Colors

  • Low Medium High not exposed (0) or not scored: the vulnerability and exposure classes on every tab.
  • Criticality quarter 0 to 3, light to dark (Overview).
  • 100-year zone 500-year zone stream crossing: flood exposure.
  • passes with room passes up to twice capacity two to four times over four times; white: record to confirm; gray: no check (Storm vs capacity).

Lines and symbols

  • Roads solid, paths dashed; culverts circles, bridges squares, transit hubs small squares.
  • Overview: line weight is the criticality quarter once zoomed in (thin lowest, thick top). Hotspots are thick red lines.
  • Culverts and bridges draw at 1:120,000 and closer, except on Flooding, where they draw at every scale.
  • Hazard surfaces (flood classes, burn probability, fire severity, susceptibility, avalanche zones) are optional layers, off by default.

Clicks and controls

  • Click any asset for its popup (the dictionary fields) and Add a workshop note.
  • Overview, Flooding, Landslide, Debris flow: click a culvert to draw its drainage area, orange for ground that drains straight to it, blue dashed for ground arriving through upstream culverts.
  • Wildfire: click a road or path to outline the 300 ft sampling buffer its score came from.
  • Flooding: Design storm swaps the capacity term between the 100-year storm (of record) and the 25-year. The filters (Hotspots only, High exposure only, Class 3 only, Flagged for owner review) narrow map and table together. Every table has Export CSV.
