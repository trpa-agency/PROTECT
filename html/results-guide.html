Draft for Workshop 1, Nov. 9, 2026 How each road, culvert, bridge, path, and transit hub on the Results page got its score, and how to read the map. The scores are a draft that ranks assets and raises questions; they are not design values.

Letter size, one page. Choose Save as PDF to download.

How an asset gets a score two steps, built on FHWA vulnerability assessment guidance

Step 1

Criticality: how much the asset matters

Each road segment is scored on traffic, the detour if it closed, access to critical places, and who depends on it (equity), then cut into quarters, 0 (lowest) to 3 (top). Culverts and bridges take their road's class.

Step 2

Vulnerability: how likely the asset is to fail

Fifteen hazard-asset pairs are scored for vulnerability using Vulnerability = (Exposure x weightExposure) + (Sensitivity x weightSensitivity). Exposure is the likelihood and severity of the asset's exposure to the hazard, sensitivity is how badly it would fare if exposed to the hazard; both score 0 to 3. Roads have no sensitivity term, so a road's vulnerability score is its exposure.

Classes. Low under 1, Medium 1 to under 2, High 2 and over. Wildfire scores run 1 to 3 everywhere, so wildfire uses Low under 1.5, Medium 1.5 to 2, High over 2.
Combined class. An asset in several pairs carries its highest pair score (the popup names it as Highest hazard); its class is the highest of its pair classes.
Hotspots. High vulnerability on a road in the top criticality quarter.
Stand-in. A dataset used in place of the one the method names until it arrives. The debris-flow pairs rest on stand-ins (fire severity for soil burn severity, and a drainage-size break that puts nearly every basin at 3), so they stay out of the combined class; the "with stand-ins" fields include them.
Missing data. An unobserved sensitivity input (most culvert conditions) takes the method's default; Sensitivity data then reads partial or default.

Reading the map

Colors

  • Low Medium High not exposed (0) or not scored: the vulnerability and exposure classes on every tab.
  • Criticality quarter 0 to 3, dark to bright, on a dark basemap (Overview).
  • 100-year zone 500-year zone stream crossing: flood exposure.
  • passes with room passes up to twice capacity two to four times over four times; white: record to confirm; gray: no check (Storm vs capacity).

Lines and symbols

  • Roads solid, paths dashed; culverts circles, bridges squares, transit hubs small squares.
  • Overview: line weight is the criticality quarter once zoomed in (thin lowest, thick top). Hotspots are thick red lines.
  • Culverts and bridges draw at 1:120,000 and closer, except on Flooding, where they draw at every scale.
  • Hazard surfaces (flood classes, burn probability, fire severity, susceptibility, avalanche zones) are optional layers, off by default.

Clicks and controls

  • Click any asset for its popup (the dictionary fields) and Add a workshop note.
  • Overview, Flooding, Landslide, Debris flow: click a culvert to draw its drainage area, orange for ground that drains straight to it, blue dashed for ground arriving through upstream culverts.
  • Wildfire: click a road or path to outline the 300 ft sampling buffer its score came from.
  • Flooding: Design storm swaps the capacity term between the 100-year storm (of record) and the 25-year. The filters (Hotspots only, High exposure only, Class 3 only, Flagged for owner review) narrow map and table together. Every table has Export CSV.

Every field in the popups, tables, and exports is defined in the data dictionary on the References page, with a glossary and the data sources. Go further: the Scoring rubrics (every indicator, weight, break, and decision) and the Criticality page (step 1). All at trpa-agency.github.io/PROTECT/html/.
