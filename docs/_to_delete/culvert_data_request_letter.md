Dear [Recipient Name]:

The Tahoe Regional Planning Agency (TRPA), in its capacity as the federally designated Tahoe Metropolitan Planning Organization (TMPO), has been awarded a Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) Promoting Resilient Operations for Transformative, Efficient, and Cost-saving Transportation (PROTECT) Discretionary Grant to develop a Lake Tahoe Region Risk Index and Resilience Improvement Plan (RIRIP). The Plan will quantify exposure and vulnerability for surface transportation assets and the communities they serve across the bi-state Lake Tahoe Basin and translate the results into a prioritized portfolio of capital and operational investments eligible for PROTECT formula and discretionary funding.

Drainage crossings — culverts in particular — are among the most consequential transportation assets in the Basin from a resilience standpoint. Undersized, aging, or scour-vulnerable culverts are the dominant failure mode in our recent flood, atmospheric river, and post-fire debris flow events, and the data we maintain in-house is not yet sufficient to score them at the asset level. Bridge condition is well covered by the National Bridge Inventory; culvert inventories, however, are owned and maintained by the individual jurisdictions responsible for the roads on which the culverts sit. TRPA is therefore reaching out to each jurisdiction that owns, maintains, or inspects culverts within the Lake Tahoe Basin to request a data share.

## What We Are Requesting

We are requesting the most current available culvert inventory for assets located within the Lake Tahoe Basin (or, where boundaries are not natively tracked, for the relevant district, county, or forest unit covering the Basin). For each culvert, the following attributes are most useful to the Risk Index, in approximate order of priority:

1. Location (point or line geometry, with horizontal accuracy noted)
2. Owner and maintenance responsibility
3. Material, shape, and inside dimensions (rise / span / diameter)
4. Installation year or estimated age
5. Most recent condition rating and date of last inspection
6. Hydraulic capacity (design storm return period, if available)
7. Scour vulnerability or known scour history
8. Aquatic Organism Passage (AOP) status or attribution, where tracked
9. Any known failures, replacements, or known-deficient flags

Partial inventories are useful. If your jurisdiction tracks a subset of the above, please share what you have; we can supplement from other sources and will not attempt to backfill missing attributes without your input. We are equally interested in tabular data, geospatial data (Esri File Geodatabase, GeoPackage, GeoJSON, Shapefile, or REST endpoint), or PDFs of inspection reports if that is the form in which the data is maintained.

## How TRPA Will Use the Data

The culvert data shared will be used by TRPA and TRPA's PROTECT consultant team (ICF) for the Risk Index and Resilience Improvement Plan. Any derivative products — for example, risk scores at the asset or segment level, or prioritized investment recommendations — will be attributed to the source data and shared back with the contributing jurisdictions in advance of public release. TRPA will respect any data-handling restrictions you specify (for example, omitting precise locations from public-facing tools, redacting condition ratings, or limiting redistribution).

TRPA is happy to execute a written data-sharing agreement, Memorandum of Understanding, or letter of acknowledgment if that is your jurisdiction's preferred path. We can also receive data under your existing public records protocols.

## Mutual Benefit

The PROTECT Risk Index will produce, at a minimum, the following products that may be useful to your jurisdiction:

- Asset-segment-level scoring of climate hazard exposure (wildfire, post-fire debris flow, flood, geohazards, wind, seiche) across the Basin transportation network
- Adaptive-capacity scoring including network redundancy and detour ratios
- Prioritized investment portfolios eligible for PROTECT formula and discretionary funding, including projects that benefit the contributing jurisdiction
- A regional resilience improvement plan that can be incorporated into your jurisdiction's capital planning, hazard mitigation plan, or asset management plan

TRPA welcomes the opportunity to coordinate with your team on data interpretation and on any culvert-related findings before they are published.

## Timeline and Next Steps

We are working toward a Phase 1 Risk Index Tool release in mid-summer 2026 and a full Resilience Improvement Plan in early 2027. Data received by July 31, 2026 can be reliably integrated into the Phase 1 release; data received after that date will be incorporated into the full Plan.
